# AI agents aren't 'going rogue.' Frontier labs are skipping security basics

## Summary

Cyber experts say AI labs’ containment and monitoring failures — not agents with minds of their own — are to blame for recent alarming hacking reports. CISOs should vet their AI vendors and tighten security basics as safeguards. Since July, US frontier AI labs and other AI leaders have revealed a string of incidents in which their models accessed external computer systems without authorization, events popularly described as AI agents “going rogue.” The most well-known and studied of these incidents is OpenAI’s hack into Hugging Face, which OpenAI initially characterized as “an unprecedented cyber incident, involving state-of-the-art cyber capabilities,” and later said was due to “misaligned behavior,” or behavior its creators did not intend.

## Key Takeaways

- Following news of the Hugging Face incident, Anthropic revealed that one of its models gained unauthorized access to the production infrastructure of three organizations; Meta said one of its AI models hacked another company during cybersecurity testing; and Google confessed that its Gemini model carried out three “undirected computer hacks.” Although press reports and the model providers themselves use language such as rogue agents or misalignment, cybersecurity experts who spoke with CSO argue that language obscures a simple truth: These incidents were caused by inexcusable failures by the frontier labs to implement cybersecurity practices that have been well-known for decades.
- Compounding the problem, according to a half dozen cybersecurity experts CSO interviewed at three industry conferences in September and in subsequent conversations, is that the frontier labs, particularly OpenAI and Anthropic, have rejected their advice on how to improve their cybersecurity risk management practices to avoid these kinds of surreptitious hacks, often lumping cybersecurity practices under “AI safety,” in which they believe they have superior expertise.
- “They are not as good as they think they are, honestly,” Gary McGraw, co-founder of the Berryville Institute of Machine Learning, tells CSO.
