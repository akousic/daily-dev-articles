## Summary

Closing the Engineering the Developer Experience track at QCon San Francisco 2026, Ankit Jain, co-founder and CEO of Aviator, argued that line-by-line code review is already quietly dead on most teams, and that the sharper question is "how long until we stop reading the code?" He also flagged his conflict of interest up front, noting that he runs a company that sells a solution and asking the audience to "be suspicious of me." The talk revisited an essay he wrote for Latent Space in March 2026 proposing a five-layer trust model to replace reviews. Eight months on, Jain said the mechanics held up but the essay missed human judgment entirely. He began with the data that unseated his original framing.

## Key Takeaways

- A 2013 Microsoft study found 44% of reviewers cited defect-finding as their motivation, yet only 14% of their comments were actually about defects, and a 2018 Google study of more than nine million reviews reached almost the same split.
- Code review was never really about bugs, he argued, it was about knowledge sharing, echoing Addy Osmani: "We made writing cheap, and understanding stayed exactly as expensive as it has always been." A September Faros study of more than 4,000 teams found that over 75% of PRs now merge without any human review, average PR size has grown, and redo work is up 67% year over year.
- Earlier research showed review quality drops sharply past 400 lines, yet the median PR today is 640 lines.
