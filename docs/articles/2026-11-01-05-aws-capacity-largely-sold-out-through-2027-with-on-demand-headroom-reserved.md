## Summary

Amazon CEO Andy Jassy recently said that AWS’s large committed cloud capacity is largely sold out through 2027, with a good chunk of 2028 already allocated, while the company continues to reserve headroom for On Demand, EC2 and Bedrock. Jassy made the remarks in a video podcast with Andreessen Horowitz co-founder Ben Horowitz and general partner Erik Torenberg, reported by Reuters on October 23. AWS sells capacity assurance through On-Demand Capacity Reservations and Capacity Blocks for ML, where a customer reserves instances in a named Availability Zone, and in the Capacity Blocks case, for a fixed window beginning on a future date.

## Key Takeaways

- As previously reported on InfoQ, AWS raised Capacity Blocks for ML rates by about 15% uniformly across every region in January, covering P5en, P5e, P5 and P4d GPU instances along with Trn2 and Trn1.
- Capacity for 2026 is already gone, Jassy said, and the company could invest well beyond the roughly $220 billion in capital expenditure planned for this year without it being a poor use of capital.
- On the allocation itself: 2027 is largely sold out.
