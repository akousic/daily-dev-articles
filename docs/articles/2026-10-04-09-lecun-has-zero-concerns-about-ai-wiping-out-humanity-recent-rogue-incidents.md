## Summary

Yann LeCun won the 2018 Turing Award—computer science’s equivalent of the Nobel Prize—along with fellow AI researchers Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio for their work on deep learning, the neural network technology that underpins today’s AI boom. But today, nearly a decade later, LeCun is the only one of the three AI “godfathers” who is not deeply concerned about the risks of artificial intelligence. In fact, he thinks many of those risks are overblown, and that the constant warning about them from executives at some of the leading AI companies is misguided and counterproductive.

## Key Takeaways

- LeCun isn’t worried “at all” about AI wiping out humanity, and he has “zero concerns” about the recent string of rogue AI incidents, including OpenAI’s agents autonomously hacking Hugging Face in July.
- He attributes the incidents to poor human oversight and system design, and says they’re “totally preventable,” a belief also held by U.S.
- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who this month called the Hugging Face incident the “responsibility of OpenAI management.” “Those agents are doing exactly what they’ve been asked to do,” LeCun said.
